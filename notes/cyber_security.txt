Cyber security is the practice of protecting computers, networks, programs and data from attacks, damage and unauthorized access.
We need to study cyber security because almost everything we do now depends on the internet, such as banking, shopping, education and communication.
As more data is stored online, attackers have more chances to steal money, personal information and business secrets.
A security failure can cause financial loss, privacy loss, damage to reputation, and even danger to national security.
Cybercrime is any illegal activity in which a computer, network or digital device is used as a tool, as a target, or both.
In India, cybercrimes are mainly handled under the Information Technology Act, 2000.
Hacking means gaining unauthorized access to a computer system or network.
Phishing is sending fake emails or messages to trick people into giving passwords, OTPs or bank details.
Identity theft means stealing someone's personal information to pretend to be them, usually for fraud.
Malware is harmful software such as viruses, worms and trojans. Ransomware is malware that locks a victim's files and demands money to unlock them.
A denial of service attack floods a website or server with traffic so that real users cannot use it.
Cyber stalking and cyber bullying mean harassing or threatening a person repeatedly through the internet or social media.
Online financial fraud includes credit card fraud, fake online shops and UPI scams.
Other types of cybercrime include software piracy, data theft, and cyber terrorism, which is using the internet to cause fear or damage to a nation.
