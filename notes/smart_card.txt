A smart card is a plastic card with a small embedded chip that can store and process data securely.
The chip is a tiny computer, so a smart card is an embedded system. It has a CPU, ROM, RAM, EEPROM and an I/O interface.
ROM stores the card's operating system. EEPROM stores user data such as keys and balances, and it keeps the data without power. RAM holds temporary working data.
A smart card has no battery. The card reader supplies its power and clock signal.
Contact smart cards have metal pads on the chip and must be inserted into a reader. They follow the ISO/IEC 7816 standard.
Contactless smart cards have an antenna coil inside. They get power from the reader's radio waves and work when tapped near it. They follow the ISO/IEC 14443 standard.
Memory cards only store data and have no processor. Microprocessor cards can run programs and do encryption, so they are more secure.
The card and the reader talk using command and response messages called APDUs.
Security features include PIN checks, encryption and tamper resistance, which make the stored data hard to copy.
Common uses are SIM cards, bank cards, ID cards, metro and transit cards, and health cards.
